THE ESSENTIALS

AI basics, made simple.

Three ideas to remember before trying an AI tool.

01

What is AI?

Technology that can recognise patterns, understand language, make predictions and help create content.

02

What is a prompt?

The instruction you give an AI tool. A better instruction usually produces a clearer first draft.

03

What should you check?

Facts, missing information, privacy, suitable wording and whether an authorised officer must review it.

DEMONSTRATION 01

Summarise a consumer complaint

Turn a fictional consumer message into a structured first draft.

TEXT AI
FICTIONAL COMPLAINT FROM THE SLIDES

“I bought a rice cooker for LKR 18,500 on 12 October. It stopped heating after three days. I have a receipt and a 12-month warranty.”

No real consumer records are used in this exercise.
⌘ COPY-PASTE PROMPT
You are assisting with initial consumer complaint review. Use only the fictional complaint below.

Create a table with these headings:
1. Product
2. Purchase date and price
3. Reported problem
4. Available evidence
5. Missing information

Do not invent facts or make legal conclusions. Write “Not provided” where information is missing.

Complaint: “I bought a rice cooker for LKR 18,500 on 12 October. It stopped heating after three days. I have a receipt and a 12-month warranty.”
What to check:Rice cooker • stopped heating after three days • receipt • 12-month warranty • model, serial number and requested outcome not provided.

Show the original English and both draft translations. Keep the meaning unchanged. Do not add any legal or official claims. State that the translations require review by a fluent, qualified person before publication.
What to check:Meaning, spelling, tone and suitability in both languages. The example translations are drafts, not approved official wording.
DEMONSTRATION 04

Spot a confident AI mistake

Learn when AI should not give an exact answer without reliable evidence.

VERIFY FIRST
UNSAFE QUESTION

“What exact penalty must CAA impose in every expired-product case?”

SAFER APPROACH

Check the current law, specific case facts, official sources and authorised interpretation before deciding.

⌘ SAFER FOLLOW-UP PROMPT
Explain why an exact penalty cannot be assumed for every expired-product case. List the types of case facts and official legal sources an authorised officer would need to verify.

Do not invent a penalty, a legal provision or a case outcome. If you cannot verify a claim, say so clearly.
Main lesson:A convincing AI answer is not automatically a correct or legally valid answer.

Write a better complaint prompt

Use this fictional kettle case from the slides:

A customer bought a kettle for LKR 7,500 on 4 October 2026. After one week, the lid would not close. The customer has a receipt. The seller said the item can only be exchanged if the box is available. The customer asks for advice.

Ask AI for an accurate summary, missing evidence and three neutral follow-up questions. Avoid guesses and legal conclusions.

WORKSHOP 02

Create a consumer awareness prompt

Topic: safe online shopping. Audience: first-time online consumers. Format: 90 words and three short tips. Guardrail: no invented law, number or official claim.

View a copy-ready example ＋
EXAMPLE PROMPT
You are helping to prepare a consumer education draft. Write a friendly 90-word message for first-time online shoppers in Sri Lanka.

Give exactly three short, practical tips about checking seller details, total prices and delivery terms, and keeping receipts or order confirmations. Use simple, public-friendly English. Do not invent laws, official contact numbers or CAA claims. The result is only a draft for human review.
PROMPT PRACTICE

Turn a weak prompt into a useful one

VAGUE

“Write something about consumer rights.”

CLEARER

“Draft a 100-word easy-English notice for first-time Sri Lankan online shoppers. Give 3 tips. Do not invent legal rules.”

To improve a draft, follow up with: “Make the wording simpler”, “Use three bullets”, or “Flag statements needing official verification”.

BEFORE YOU USE AI

A simple safety checklist.

Based on the information-handling and human-review reminders in the session.

GREEN — SUITABLE FOR PRACTICE

Public information and entirely fictional examples.

AMBER — ASK FIRST

Internal, non-public material: seek the required approval.

RED — DO NOT PASTE INTO PUBLIC TOOLS

Personal information, complaints with identifiers, investigation records, legal and confidential data.

✓

Always verify before using an AI result. AI can help draft or organise information. An authorised officer must check evidence, apply the correct rules and approve any official decision or public communication.
